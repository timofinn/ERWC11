3.07 | Make Their Case

Purpose

Anyone can trash a character. That’s easy.

A good lawyer can also defend them. That’s hard.

Today you do both to the same person.

It’s practice for the mock trial.

I. The Move

4 min · Box 1 · Left page

Copy both definitions.

Strawman
Twisting or shrinking the other side’s argument so it’s easier to attack. You beat a scarecrow, not the person.
Steelman
Building the strongest version of the other side’s argument before you take it apart. You beat them at their best.

Lawyers use both. A strawman gets a cheap laugh from the jury. A steelman earns their trust.

II. Mr. Finn’s Model

8 min · Box 2 · Left page

Reverend Hale is on trial. Same man, two lawyers. Copy both.

Prosecution · Strawman

Members of the jury, Reverend Hale cares more about being right than being good. He treated Salem as a stage for his expertise, then walked out the second the performance went badly.

What it does: shrinks Hale into a cartoon, all ego and no reasons, so the jury convicts the cartoon.

Defense · Steelman

Members of the jury, Hale came to heal a sick child, and he warned the town to trust evidence over rumor. When he saw the girls were lying, he risked his career to say so. Danforth never admitted he was wrong. Hale did.

What it does: reads Hale’s choices in the most generous light. The jury sees a man trying to do right, not a villain.

Try it out loud: as a class, prosecute Elizabeth with a strawman, then defend her with a steelman. No writing. Ask: would Elizabeth agree with the defense?

III. Watch

20 min · Just watch

Watch the film from Hale’s visit to Elizabeth’s arrest. Follow Scenes 3 and 4 below.

While you watch: John or Mary Warren. Which one would you rather put on trial? That’s your character for Boxes 3 and 4.

Scene 3 · Hale makes a house call. Reverend Hale stops by to warn them that Elizabeth was named in court, and to test them. Why does John skip church? Can he list the Ten Commandments? John gets nine. The one he forgets is adultery. Elizabeth has to fill it in. (Oof.) John tells Hale that Abigail admitted none of it was witchcraft. Hale asks him to say that in court. Then Giles Corey and Francis Nurse burst in: Martha Corey and Rebecca Nurse have been arrested.

Scene 4 · The needle. Court officers Cheever and Herrick show up with a warrant for Elizabeth. Cheever finds the poppet with a needle inside it. Here’s why that matters: Abigail was stabbed with a needle at dinner at Parris’s house, and she says Elizabeth’s spirit did it. Mary admits she made the doll in court and put the needle in herself, with Abigail sitting right next to her. Doesn’t matter. They take Elizabeth anyway. John tells Mary she has to testify against Abigail. Mary is terrified the girls will turn on her. And she knows about the affair.

IV. Prosecute

5 min · Box 3 · Right page

Prosecution · Strawman
Members of the jury, …

You’re the prosecutor. In 2 to 3 sentences, make your character look as bad as possible: one ugly motive, no good reasons.

V. Defend

8 min · Box 4 · Right page · Partners

Defense · Steelman
Members of the jury, …

Switch sides. Defend the same character with their real reasons, the most generous reading of what they did. Use one moment from the play.

  1. Write it. 5 min
  2. Read it to a partner. They answer one question: would your character agree? If not, fix one thing. 3 min
No villains allowed. If your defense makes them sound dumb or evil, you wrote another strawman.

VI. One Honest Line

4 min · Box 5 · Right page

It was easier to be the , because .

Which lawyer was easier to be? The easy side is usually the one you’d already picked.

For the brave: Elizabeth says John is judging himself harder than she is. Steelman that. Is she right?
