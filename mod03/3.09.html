3.09 | The People v. John Proctor

❖ ❖ ❖

Purpose

You watched Salem put its neighbors on trial. Now it’s Proctor’s turn.

The charge: he could have stopped the trials, and he chose his name instead.

Your job is to argue it with evidence, power, and rhetoric.

Who wins doesn’t matter. How you argue does.

Summative · 30 points · counts in the 40% Summative category
Prep cardPrinted. Due end of class Tue 11/17
TrialWed 11/18 or Thu 11/19, depending on your cast

I. Your Role

Fri 11/13 · Role card

  1. Get your role card from Mr. Finn. You don’t pick. Trust the process.
  2. Find your side: Prosecution (he’s responsible) or Defense (he’s not).
  3. Read what your side needs you to show. That’s your whole case in one line.
RoleYour job
AttorneyOpen, question witnesses, or close. 90 seconds each.
WitnessAnswer questions in character. You help write them, so no surprises. Except on cross.
JuryEveryone not on trial that day. You watch, write, and vote.

II. The Three Skills

Mon 11/16 · Prep card, boxes 1 to 3

Mr. Finn models an opening first. Watch for all three.

SkillWhat it sounds like
EvidenceA specific moment from the play. Not “Proctor was bad.” When was he bad?
PowerName the form and how it worked. Or why Proctor didn’t use it.
RhetoricSteelman the other side, or catch them in a trap (Circle or Either/Or).
The six forms: Legitimate (the title) · Reward (the treat) · Expert (the knowledge) · Referent (the fans) · Coercive (the threat) · Informational (the secret)

How lawyers use the two moves:

MoveIn courtThe risk
Strawman“The defense wants you to believe anyone who gets scared has a license to kill.”Get caught twisting words and the jury stops trusting you.
Steelman“Their alibi looks compelling on paper. Here’s why it falls apart.”Build their case too well and you win it for them.

III. Build It

Tue 11/17 · Prep card, box 4

  1. Write your lines in box 4. Attorneys: your speech or your questions. Witnesses: your answers.
  2. Rehearse out loud with your side. Twice. Your mouth needs practice too.
  3. Get stamped at the door. No stamp, no card.
Absent on trial day? You give your turn to Mr. Finn at office hours or Mon 11/30. Your prep card still counts.

Success Looks Like

  • Prep card: evidence, power, and rhetoric, in full sentences 15 pts
  • Live turn: you said it, and you used your prep card 10 pts
  • Jury sheet: one steelman and one trap you caught 5 pts
