3.00 | A Power Play: The Crucible

3.09 | Trial Timeline

Who talks when, and what you're there to do

◆ ◆ ◆

The Task

Get the audience into the show. You're practicing persuasion and performance, not proving the truth.

Your Job

One monologue, about 60 seconds, with your best point and your best delivery. Reading it flat is the one way to fail.

Pro Tips

Prosecution: Use their own words and actions as evidence. Take a different angle from your partner.

Defense: Show their humanity. Use rhetorical questions and dramatic pauses.

Character: Clear your name, and say who's really to blame.

I. How You're Scored

10 points, same three skills for every role. Scripts in hand are fine. We grade how you say it, not what you memorized.

Voice and Articulation

4 pts

Full pointsEvery word clear, pitch rises and falls, slows and pauses on the big lines.

Costs youMumbled, flat or rushed.

Body and Delivery

3 pts

Full pointsStands tall, gestures and moves with the words.

Costs youFrozen, fidgeting or hiding behind the page.

Commitment and Audience Connection

3 pts

Full pointsStays in it start to finish, eyes on the jury, owns the room.

Costs youBreaks character, giggles or talks to the page.

9 to 10 is an A, 8 a B, 7 a C, 6 a D. The verdict doesn't count. Lose the case, keep the A.

II. The Order

Every speaker gets 40 to 80 seconds. Speeches only, no questions.

  1. 1

    Judge

    Mr. Finn reads the charges.

  2. 2

    Prosecutor 1 · Opening

    Why they're guilty. Your best proof.

  3. 3

    Defender 1 · Opening

    Why they're not. Same facts, fairer light.

  4. 4

    The Character · Clear your name

    Who's really to blame. Then plead not guilty.

    No one playing the character? Skip this. Steps 5 and 6 become rebuttals: save your best line for last.

  5. 5

    Prosecutor 2 · Closing

    A different reason they're guilty.

  6. 6

    Defender 2 · Final word

    The line the jury walks out with.

  7. 7

    Jury

    Mr. Finn calls the vote. Write why on your Jury Sheet.
