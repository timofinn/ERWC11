3.00 | A Power Play: The Crucible

Mock Trial Model

The People v. Abigail Williams

◆ ◆ ◆

MODEL: the same trial, run twice. Trial A lands in the A range. Trial B lands in C/D territory. Every move is highlighted. Speeches are trimmed so each reads in about three minutes. Real ones run the full time.

I. Color It

EvidenceNames a moment and quotes the line
PowerNames the source and shows it working
SteelmanSays the other side's best point, then answers it
ThemeYour team's one sentence, said once by every speaker
Leading questionProsecution. Yes/no, so the witness can only agree
Open questionDefense. Lets the witness tell it

Missing a color? That move is missing. Time and Voice can't be colored, so the teacher notes score those. Scoring is on the Rubric.

II. How the 12 Minutes Run

  • 0:00 Judge reads the charge (30 sec)
  • 0:30 Prosecutor 1 opens (2 min)
  • 2:30 Defender 1 opens (2 min)
  • 4:30 Prosecutor 2 questions the witness (2 min)
  • 6:30 Defender 2 questions the witness (2 min)
  • 8:30 Prosecutor 2 closes, then Defender 2 closes (1 min each)
  • 10:30 Jury fills out sheets and votes (90 sec)

Table rules

  • Theme: each side writes one sentence. Every speaker says it once.
  • Questions: prosecution leads with yes/no questions. Defense asks open ones.
  • Time-out: each table gets one 30-second huddle per trial. Anyone who freezes can call it, and the timer pauses.
  • No objections: if you hear bad evidence, call it out in your own time. That's the bonus point.

III. Trial A: The A-Range Version

Prosecution theme Scared people hide. Abigail aimed. (Theme)

Defense theme The adults handed her the power. (Theme)

JudgeCourt is in session. Abigail Williams is charged with knowingly accusing innocent people of witchcraft to save herself and get Elizabeth Proctor out of the way. Prosecution, two minutes.

TimerGo.

Prosecutor 1Abigail didn't panic. She planned. In Act 1, Betty screams, "You drank a charm to kill John Proctor's wife!" (Evidence) That's before anyone says the word witch. By the end of Act 2, Elizabeth is in chains. Same target. How does Abigail keep the girls in line? "I will bring a pointy reckoning that will shudder you." (Evidence) That's coercive power. She has no title and no money, so fear is the only power she's got, and she uses it. (Power) The defense is going to say she's a scared 17-year-old orphan. Honestly? She is scared. But scared doesn't explain why every finger points at Elizabeth. (Steelman) Scared people hide. Abigail aimed. (Theme)

Teacher note: Evidence 2, two moments, both quoted. Power 2, names coercive and shows it working on the girls. Steelman 2. Theme said once, right at the end. Time 2, done at 1:50. Voice 2, eyes on the jury. 10/10

Defender 1The prosecution says she aimed. But who said witchcraft first? Not Abigail. Parris and Putnam did, and then Hale walks in with a stack of books. That's expertise power, and the whole room bows to it. (Power) Abigail is 17, no parents, and the dancing alone could hang her. So when Hale corners her, she gives him exactly what he came for: "She made me do it! She made Betty do it!" (Evidence) The prosecution is right that the charm looks bad. But wanting someone gone isn't the same as making a whole town lose its mind. (Steelman) The adults handed her the power. (Theme)

Teacher note: Evidence 2, Power 2, Steelman 2, Theme said once. Time 2. Voice 1, read most of it off the page. 9/10

Prosecutor 2Abigail, you drank a charm in the woods, didn't you? (Leading question)

Abigail(checks card) Yes.

Prosecutor 2A charm to kill Elizabeth Proctor? (Leading question)

Abigail...Yes.

Prosecutor 2Elizabeth had fired you, hadn't she? (Leading question)

AbigailShe did. "She is blackening my name in the village!"

Prosecutor 2And you told the court her spirit stabbed you with a needle. (Leading question)

AbigailI did.

Prosecutor 2Nobody else could see that spirit, could they? (Leading question)

Abigail...No.

Prosecutor 2And in Act 4, you took your uncle's money and ran, didn't you? (Leading question)

AbigailI did.

Prosecutor 2No further questions.

Teacher note: This is cross. Short yes/no questions, one fact each, so Abigail can only agree. She never gets room to tell her side. The spirit question sets up the spectral evidence catch.

Defender 2Abigail, tell the jury what happened when Reverend Hale questioned you. (Open question)

AbigailI said, "She made me do it! She made Betty do it!"

Defender 2Why did you say that? (Open question)

AbigailI was scared. Everyone was staring at me, and the grown-ups wanted a witch.

Defender 2What happened in court when Mary said you were all pretending? (Open question)

Abigail"A wind, a cold wind, has come." I saw her spirit as a bird.

Teacher note: This is direct. Open questions ("tell the jury," "why," "what happened") let Abigail tell it in her own words, so the jury hears the fear.

Teacher note on the witness: Accuracy 2, every answer is on the card. In character 2, "I" every time. Answers both sides 2, gave the prosecution the facts and the defense the fear. Time 2. Voice 2. Checking the card isn't a penalty. 10/10

Prosecutor 2 (closing)You heard her: "I said her spirit stabbed me with a needle." (Evidence) Nobody else could see that spirit. That's spectral evidence, and Salem hanged people on it. The defense says the adults pushed her. They did. But the adults didn't pick Elizabeth. Abigail did. (Steelman) Scared people hide. Abigail aimed. (Theme) Guilty.

Teacher note: Evidence 2, Steelman 2, Theme, Time 2, Voice 2. No yellow: Power 1, she implies the court's power but never names a source. Plus the spectral evidence catch. 9/10 + 1 bonus

Defender 2 (closing)Here's the prosecution's best point: the charm is real, and she wanted Elizabeth gone. We're not denying it. (Steelman) She told you herself, "the grown-ups wanted a witch." (Evidence) So where did her power come from? Hale's expertise. The court's title. The girls followed her because the adults believed her. (Power) Take away the court and she's a fired servant nobody listens to. The adults handed her the power. (Theme) Not guilty.

Teacher note: The model steelman. She says the other side's best point in plain words, admits it, then shows why it isn't enough. Evidence 2, Power 2, Steelman 2, Theme, Time 2, Voice 2. 10/10

IV. Trial B: The C/D Version

JudgeCourt is in session. Abigail Williams is charged with knowingly accusing innocent people of witchcraft to save herself and get Elizabeth Proctor out of the way. Prosecution, two minutes.

TimerGo.

Prosecutor 1Um, so Abigail is guilty because she lies the whole play. She lies to her uncle, she lies to Hale, and she threatens the girls in Act 1, like she says she'll bring a reckoning or whatever. She has a lot of power in Salem. The defense will probably say she was scared, but she wasn't. So yeah. Guilty.

Teacher note: No colors at all. Evidence 1, real moments but no quote. Power 1, "a lot of power" names nothing. Steelman 1, mentions the defense but just says "no." No theme. Time 1, sat down at 0:50 of 2:00. Voice 2. 6/10

Fix it: The quote is on the case file. "I will bring a pointy reckoning that will shudder you" turns that Evidence 1 into a 2.

Defender 1(reading, head down) She's a 17-year-old orphan with no title, no money and a noose waiting if the dancing comes out. The adults called it witchcraft first. When Hale asked her she said, "She made me do it! She made Betty do it!" (Evidence) Also the prosecution is just being mean to her.

Teacher note: One color. Evidence 2, a real quote. Power 1, "no title" is the right idea, but she never shows how power worked. Steelman 0, "just being mean" is a strawman. No theme. Time 2. Voice 1, read with her head down. 6/10

Prosecutor 2Abigail, what did you drink in the woods?

AbigailUm, she drank... wait. I drank, like, a potion? To make John love me?

Prosecutor 2Okay. Why did you accuse people?

AbigailBecause they're evil. (laughs) I don't know.

Prosecutor 2No further questions.

Teacher note: No green. These are open questions on cross, so Abigail rambles and nothing useful lands. Lead her instead: "You drank a charm to kill Elizabeth, didn't you?"

Defender 2Do you think you're guilty?

AbigailNo.

Defender 2Were you scared?

AbigailYeah. (long pause) The grown-ups wanted a witch.

Teacher note: No orange. Yes/no questions on direct waste your own witness. Ask "Tell the jury what happened when Hale questioned you."

Teacher note on the witness: Accuracy 1, the card says the charm was to kill Elizabeth, not to win John. In character 1, opened with "she." Answers both sides 1. Time 1, one long freeze, and nobody called the time-out. Voice 2. 6/10

Prosecutor 2 (closing)So she basically admitted it. She's guilty. The defense didn't really prove anything, so you have to vote guilty.

Teacher note: No colors. Evidence 1, points at the testimony but never says what was in it. Power 0. Steelman 1. No theme. Time 2. Voice 2. 6/10

Defender 2 (closing)She was scared, you heard her. Prosecution says she lied, and yeah she lied, but everybody in Salem lied. The grown-ups were in charge, not her. Not guilty.

Teacher note: No colors. Evidence 1, no quote. Power 1, "the grown-ups were in charge" hints at a source without naming it. Steelman 1, "everybody lied" isn't an answer. No theme. Time 2, Voice 2. 7/10

V. What Made the Difference

  • Quote, don't summarize. Every blue has the actual line.
  • Name the power, then show it working. "A lot of power" is a 1 every time.
  • Say their best point before you beat it. "They're being mean" is a 0.
  • One theme, every speaker. Trial A's jury hears the same sentence four times. Trial B's hears nothing that ties together.
  • Lead on cross, open on direct. Trial A's prosecutor controls Abigail. Trial B's lets her ramble.
